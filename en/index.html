Preamble

Why this event?

The Scientific Day on Optimisation Ecosystems (JSEO) follows directly from our own work and experience with approximate methods applied to hard optimisation problems.

Used on its own, a metaheuristic, however effective it may be on certain classes of problems, quickly reaches its limits when faced with the growing diversity and complexity of today's optimisation problems.

It therefore becomes essential to adopt more robust collective systems, structured around cooperation and collaboration between exact methods (EM), heuristics (H) and metaheuristics (MH), within distributed and parallel architectures.

Built on a new form of algorithmic hybridisation and coordinated by artificial intelligence, this paradigm marks a shift towards more intelligent participatory models. By drawing on the synergy of their respective properties, these collective approaches overcome the limits inherent in any single method, moving optimisation away from the isolated algorithm and towards a genuine algorithmic ecosystem that is efficient, robust and high performing.

It is precisely this shift that the JSEO sets out to explore, through the study of collaborative approaches that strengthen the efficiency, robustness and adaptability of approximate methods.

Objectives

What we set out to build

Bringing together researchers, doctoral students and practitioners around a shared question: how do we design, formalise and deploy genuinely collaborative ecosystems of metaheuristics, and what concrete benefits can we expect from them, in theory as well as in practice?

  1. Survey the state of the art in hybrid and collaborative approaches across exact methods, heuristics and metaheuristics.
  2. Encourage exchange between the optimisation, artificial intelligence and applied engineering communities.
  3. Highlight concrete use cases where combining several algorithms made the difference.
  4. Identify the scientific obstacles that remain open and sketch out future avenues for collaboration.
  5. Build a lasting bridge between those who use optimisation techniques.
  6. Give early career researchers, master's and doctoral students a platform to present and discuss their work.
Thematic areas

Four areas, one shared question

Contributions are organised around four thematic areas, given as an indication and not as a restriction.

Area 1

Intelligent hybridisation of optimisation methods

New hybridisation strategies across heuristics, metaheuristics and exact methods, matheuristics and automatic method selection.

Area 2

Intelligent cooperation and collaboration (MetaStorming)

Methods that interact, exchange and reinforce one another: multi-agent systems, distributed optimisation, reproducibility.

Area 3

Learning, adaptation and intelligence of ecosystems

When artificial intelligence guides the search: self-configuration, reinforcement learning, dynamic orchestration.

Area 4

Optimisation and decision intelligence

Industrial, socio-economic and interdisciplinary applications, with particular attention to real data and working prototypes.
